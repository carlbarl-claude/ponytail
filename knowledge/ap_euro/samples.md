# AP European History — Illustrative Scored Samples

> **These are original, synthetic teaching examples — NOT official College Board
> student samples.** They're written to show clearly *why* a response does or
> doesn't earn a given point. To study real scored samples, download the official
> ones (see `knowledge/sources.md`) and upload them to the bot.

## DBQ Thesis — earns the point (1/1)
**Prompt (paraphrased):** Evaluate the extent to which the Protestant Reformation
changed the relationship between Church and state in the 16th century.

**Response:** "Although the medieval Church had long claimed authority over secular
rulers, the Reformation decisively shifted power toward the state: German princes
and monarchs like Henry VIII used religious reform to seize Church lands and assert
control over religious life, making the state, not Rome, the dominant authority in
much of northern Europe."

**Why it earns the point:** It takes a clear, defensible position ("decisively
shifted power toward the state"), responds directly to "extent," and previews a line
of reasoning (princes/monarchs using reform to gain power). It's arguable, not just
a restatement of the prompt.

## DBQ Thesis — does NOT earn the point (0/1)
**Response:** "The Protestant Reformation was a very important event that changed
many things about religion and politics in Europe during the 16th century."

**Why it fails:** No defensible *argument* and no line of reasoning — it just asserts
importance and restates the prompt's topic. "Changed many things" isn't a position a
historian could disagree with, so there's nothing to defend.

## DBQ Sourcing / HIPP — earns credit
**Document:** A 1525 pamphlet by a Lutheran preacher urging peasants to reject
Church authority.

**Response:** "Because the author is a Lutheran preacher writing during the German
Peasants' War (historical situation) and aiming to persuade common people to break
from Rome (purpose), his claim that the Church oppressed ordinary Christians is
likely exaggerated to rally support — so it reveals reformist rhetoric more than a
neutral description of Church policy."

**Why it earns credit:** It doesn't just name the author — it explains *why* the
purpose and historical situation make us read the document a certain way, and ties
that to the argument.

## DBQ Sourcing / HIPP — does NOT earn credit
**Response:** "This document was written by a Lutheran preacher in 1525."

**Why it fails:** Pure identification. It states who and when but never explains how
the point of view, purpose, situation, or audience is *relevant to the argument*.

## LEQ — Complexity point, earned
**Topic:** Causes of the French Revolution.

**Response excerpt:** "While Enlightenment ideas provided a language of rights that
revolutionaries used, they alone did not cause the Revolution: it was the collision
of fiscal crisis, a rigid social hierarchy, and a weak monarchy that turned
philosophy into revolt. Indeed, many nobles read the same Enlightenment texts yet
defended their privileges — showing that ideas mattered only when material grievances
made people receptive to them."

**Why it earns complexity:** It analyzes *multiple* variables (ideas, finances,
social structure, weak monarchy), qualifies a common claim (ideas weren't sufficient
on their own), and corroborates with a specific counter-example (nobles reading the
same texts). That's sophistication sustained across the argument, not a throwaway
line.

## SAQ — Part answered well vs. poorly
**Task:** "Explain ONE way the Scientific Revolution challenged traditional
authority in the 17th century."

**Earns the point:** "The Scientific Revolution challenged the authority of the
Church because figures like Galileo used telescopic observation and mathematics to
support heliocentrism, which contradicted the Church-endorsed geocentric model and
implied that truth came from empirical evidence rather than religious doctrine."
(*Specific figure + specific mechanism + explains the challenge.*)

**Fails:** "The Scientific Revolution made people question authority because of new
scientific discoveries." (*True but vague — no specific evidence and no explained
"how."*)
