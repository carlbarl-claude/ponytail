# AP European History — Scoring Rubrics (summary)

These are plain-language summaries of the official AP History scoring criteria,
written to help students understand what graders reward. Point values reflect the
current AP History rubrics. For the exact official wording, see the College Board
scoring guidelines linked in `knowledge/sources.md`.

## DBQ — Document-Based Question (7 points total)

### DBQ · Thesis / Claim (1 pt)
Respond to the prompt with a historically defensible thesis or claim that
establishes a **line of reasoning**. It must do more than restate the prompt: it
should make an argument and preview *how* you'll defend it. The thesis must be one
or more sentences located in the introduction or conclusion.

### DBQ · Contextualization (1 pt)
Describe a **broader historical context** relevant to the prompt — events,
developments, or processes before, during, or after the prompt's timeframe. This
must be more than a passing phrase; it typically takes a few sentences that set the
stage. Connect the specific topic to bigger developments.

### DBQ · Evidence from the Documents (up to 2 pts)
- **1 pt:** Use the *content* of at least **three** documents to address the topic.
- **2 pts:** Support an argument in response to the prompt using at least **six**
  documents. (Merely quoting isn't enough for the second point — the documents must
  actively support *your argument*.)

### DBQ · Evidence beyond the Documents (1 pt)
Use at least **one** additional piece of specific historical evidence (beyond what's
in the documents) that is relevant to the argument. It must be a concrete
example — a specific event, person, or development — not a vague reference, and it
must be different from the contextualization.

### DBQ · Analysis: Sourcing / HIPP (1 pt)
For at least **three** documents, explain **how or why** the document's point of
view, purpose, historical situation, and/or audience is relevant to the argument
(remember it as **HIPP**: Historical situation, Intended audience, Purpose, Point
of view). Don't just identify the author — explain why that sourcing *matters* for
how we read the document.

### DBQ · Complexity (1 pt)
Demonstrate a **complex understanding** of the topic, using evidence. Ways to earn
it: explain nuance by analyzing multiple variables; explain both similarity and
difference, or both continuity and change, or multiple causes; consider diverse
perspectives; or corroborate/qualify an argument with evidence across the essay.
This point rewards genuine sophistication woven throughout, not a single sentence.

## LEQ — Long Essay Question (6 points total)

### LEQ · Thesis / Claim (1 pt)
Same standard as the DBQ thesis: a historically defensible claim that responds to
the prompt and establishes a line of reasoning.

### LEQ · Contextualization (1 pt)
Describe a broader historical context relevant to the prompt (same standard as the
DBQ contextualization point).

### LEQ · Evidence (up to 2 pts)
- **1 pt:** Provide at least **two** specific, relevant examples of evidence.
- **2 pts:** Use that evidence to **support an argument** in response to the prompt.

### LEQ · Analysis and Reasoning (up to 2 pts)
- **1 pt:** Use a **historical reasoning process** — comparison, causation, or
  continuity and change over time (CCOT) — to frame or structure the argument.
- **2 pts:** Demonstrate a **complex understanding** (same idea as DBQ complexity).

## SAQ — Short-Answer Question (3 points total)
Each SAQ has three parts (A, B, C), worth **1 point each**. No thesis is required —
just answer the specific task. Common verbs and what they demand:
- **Identify / Describe:** state a specific, accurate example or feature.
- **Explain:** give the *how* or *why* — a reason or causal link, not just a fact.
Be specific and concise. A vague or off-topic answer earns nothing even if it's true.

## The four AP History reasoning skills
- **Causation:** analyze causes and/or effects; distinguish cause from correlation.
- **Comparison:** analyze similarities and differences and *why* they exist.
- **Continuity and Change Over Time (CCOT):** analyze what changed and what stayed
  the same across a period, and why.
- **Contextualization:** situate events within a broader historical setting.
